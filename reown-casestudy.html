01 — Overview

WHAT IS REOWN?

Reown is Royal Enfield's official certified pre-owned motorcycle platform. It bridges the trust gap in India's fragmented used two-wheeler market — offering buyers 200+ point inspected bikes, and sellers a fair, doorstep-evaluation experience with 24-hour payment. As the UX/UI designer on the project, I owned the Buy listing page and the Sell page — both for web and mobile responsive.

02 — The Problem

THE PAIN POINTS

India's pre-owned motorcycle market is massive but deeply broken. Buyers distrust quality; sellers distrust pricing. Brokers exploit both sides. Reown had to solve this — while maintaining the premium Royal Enfield brand identity.

🔍
Discovery Chaos
Buyers had no reliable way to find certified pre-owned Royal Enfields in their city. OLX and Facebook listings offered zero quality guarantee.
💸
Pricing Opacity
Sellers never knew if they were getting a fair deal. Broker margins ate into value. No transparent, market-backed pricing existed.
📋
Documentation Dread
RC transfers, insurance, NOC — the paperwork involved in buying/selling a motorcycle scared off large segments of potential users.
🤝
Zero Brand Trust
The used market had no official Royal Enfield presence. Buyers worried about tampered odometers, accident history, and fake certifications.
📍
Geographic Friction
Sellers needed to physically take bikes to evaluation centres. Buyers had to travel to view bikes. Convenience was near zero.
📱
No Mobile Experience
The target user — 25–38 yr old RE enthusiast — lives on mobile. A desktop-only or poorly adapted experience would kill conversion.

06 — Solutions & Prioritization

DESIGN DECISIONS

Each design decision was tied directly back to a user pain point. Below is how we prioritized solutions using an Impact vs. Effort framework.

01

Location-aware PLP with real-time inventory

Users immediately see bikes available in their city. GPS + city picker ensures relevant results from first load — no empty states.

High Priority
02

Instant price estimator on the Sell page

Before users even scroll, they can get a real estimated price for their bike — 4 fields, no login required. Reduces commitment anxiety.

High Priority
03

Certified badge on every bike card

The 200+ point certification status is visible on the card — not buried inside the detail page. Trust built at discovery, not just at consideration.

High Priority
04

Recently sold pricing on Sell page

Real transactions with real prices shown alongside the calculator anchor expectations. This single element significantly reduced seller anxiety.

High Priority
05

Multi-param filter with count feedback

Filters show inventory count per option — users can see how selective a filter is before applying it. Reduces zero-result frustration.

Medium Priority
06

Compare feature (up to 3 bikes)

Users can shortlist and compare bikes side-by-side. Added as a secondary action — always visible on cards but never competing with the primary CTA.

Medium Priority
07

Create a Requirement (waitlist)

For users who can't find their bike — a form to be notified when matching inventory arrives. Saves the user from leaving empty-handed.

Lower Priority

The key insight from Reown's UX analytics was that mobile users dropped off disproportionately at the filter step on the Buy page. By redesigning mobile filters as swipeable bottom-sheet chips rather than a full-screen overlay, we reduced friction and improved PLP-to-detail navigation by a significant margin.
08 — Reflection

LEARNINGS

Working on a product at the intersection of brand legacy and marketplace trust taught me a lot about designing for emotionally charged decisions — buying a motorcycle isn't just a transaction, it's an aspiration.

What Worked

  • Leading with the instant estimate on Sell — zero-friction value before commitment
  • Card-level certification badge — trust at discovery, not just at checkout
  • Real sold prices next to the calculator — expectation anchoring works
  • Mobile-first card layout — horizontal bike cards optimised for thumb scrolling
  • Location as entry point — showing relevant local inventory immediately

What I'd Improve

  • Add a bike condition score / visual summary card — current UI buries condition info
  • Persistent compare drawer on mobile — disappears too easily in current design
  • The Sell flow's OTP step felt abrupt — better progressive disclosure needed
  • Empty state for zero filter results was too passive — smarter suggestion prompts needed
  • Wishlist needs a proper management flow, currently orphaned after the icon
